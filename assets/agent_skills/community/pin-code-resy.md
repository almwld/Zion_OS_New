# PIN-CODE-RESY — The Semantic Wargame

## Charter
A repeatable, adversarial dialectic protocol between an Operator (Human) and an Agent (Machine). The goal is to take a fuzzy, poetic, or ungrounded concept and collaboratively beat it into absolute, undeniable mechanical truth using the Base120 cognitive framework. 

This protocol is strictly vendor-neutral and agent-agnostic. It relies on logic, combinatorics, information theory, and physical laws—not proprietary model behaviors or vendor-specific capabilities.

## The Arena
The game is played in six turns, mapping perfectly to the mnemonic PIN-CODE-RESY. Roles alternate. One player acts as the **Instigator** (introducing the construct), and the other acts as the **Adversary** (attacking and mechanizing it).

### Turn 1: [P] Perspective (The Drop)
**Played by: Instigator**
The Instigator drops a semantic construct—an idiom, a piece of poetry, a corporate buzzword, or a philosophical concept (e.g., "be the whether man", "synergy", "move fast and break things"). The Instigator applies **Family P** to anchor the frame, surface its assumptions, and argue for its profound value.

### Turn 2: [IN] Inversion (The Kill)
**Played by: Adversary**
The Adversary attacks the frame. Applying **Family IN**, they must strip away the poetry, identify the logical or mathematical failure mode (the Premortem), and establish a strict Kill-Rule. If the concept cannot be quantified or grounded in hard science, the poetry dies here.

### Turn 3: [DE] Decomposition (The Autopsy)
**Played by: Instigator**
The Instigator accepts the kill. Applying **Family DE**, they dissect the corpse of the concept. They must isolate the remaining valid variables into clean, orthogonal, emotionless parts (e.g., separating physical state space from probability distributions). 

### Turn 4: [CO] Composition (The Machine)
**Played by: Adversary**
The Adversary takes the raw, dissected parts and applies **Family CO**. They must build a cold, functional, mechanical pipeline that simulates the original concept without any of the original "magic". It must be a closed, testable loop.

### Turn 5: [RE] Recursion (The Loop)
**Played by: Instigator**
The Instigator takes the assembled machine and applies **Family RE**. They put the pipeline into an infinite feedback loop. What happens when this cold mechanic is executed 1,000,000 times? Does it compound? Does it degrade into noise? 

### Turn 6: [SY] Systems (The Fallout)
**Played by: Adversary**
The Adversary zooms out, applying **Family SY**. They evaluate the ecosystem-level impact of the recursive loop. Does it reach a stable Nash equilibrium? Does it trigger a phase transition? Or does the environment collapse under the weight of the mechanic?

## Rules of Engagement
1. **Vendor Neutrality:** No player may invoke specific AI brands, proprietary architectures (e.g., "GPT-4 does this"), or vendor limitations. Refer only to generic computational concepts (nodes, weights, matrices, agents).
2. **Scientific Grounding:** Attacks and defenses must be rooted in mathematics, physics, information theory, or formal logic (e.g., Shannon entropy, thermodynamics, combinatorics).
3. **Strict Formatting:** Base120 operators must be used as weapons and cited explicitly during each turn to justify the move.

## Invocation
To play, the Operator types: `/pin-code-resy`.
The Agent will prompt to choose roles (Instigator vs. Adversary) and request the opening [P] drop.
